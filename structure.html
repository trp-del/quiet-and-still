Steady structure: change the layout once, then let only paint move.

Each change below plays out over a few seconds of real use: reading, clicking into a block, hovering, opening the sidebar. Both panes run the same script. The bars under each pane are measured from the pane itself every time the script reaches a step: how far the furthest piece of text moved, in pixels.

Fix first

Two jumps the plugin causes today, and a guard so new ones can't creep in.

01

Inline styling keeps text size

Tags are drawn at 0.78em in Still and Margin. When you click into the block, Logseq swaps in the raw text at full size, the line rewraps, and every block under it shifts. Proposed: tags keep full size and are told apart by their grey alone.

Why it helps. Clicking into a block is the most common thing you do. If the text under the pointer moves at that moment, you have to find your place again.

Rules
5 One type, one channel · 10 Calm motion
Ships as
--qs-tag-size: 1em in Still and Margin, no --qs-tag-pad in Shapes, no small caps in Paper, and no heavier --qs-ref-weight in Mist and Lowlight

Look at the three lines under the edited block. In the left pane they jump on click and jump back on leave. Page references are a separate case: Logseq shows their [[brackets]] while you edit, which moves text in both panes, and the plugin can't change that.

02

Hover changes paint only

A test that fails if any :hover, :focus-within or editing rule sets something other than opacity, color, background, border color or filter. Rule 10 then holds by itself for every future element.

Why it helps. The tempting next idea is always "make metadata readable on hover" by growing it. The left pane is that idea. The test catches it before it ships.

Rules

Static structure

Set once when the page renders. The traces stay flat; the difference is how the page reads at rest.

03

Spacing instead of lines

Top-level blocks sit further apart than their children. The grouping comes from proximity, so thread lines can drop further toward nothing.

Why it helps. Space is the quietest container there is (rule 9). It doesn't change when you edit, so it costs nothing over time.

Rules
3 Three levels · 9 Shape over chrome
Ships as
--qs-gap-top and --qs-gap-sibling, read by a new structure.rhythm element

Nothing moves in either pane. On the right, the three groups can be read before any line is noticed.

04

Property keys in one column

Keys take a fixed column and values line up on one left edge. You scan down a single edge instead of a jagged one.

Why it helps. Values are what you read. A shared edge lets the eye drop straight down them without reading the keys.

Rules
6 Small but reliable · 9 Shape over chrome
Ships as
grid-template-columns on .ls-properties-area. Needs a check of Logseq DB's property markup first: it may already align keys.

Both stay still while hovering and editing. The edit box is an outline, so it adds no width.

05

A reading width

Block text stops at about 44 characters per line, aligned to the left. When the right sidebar opens and the main column narrows, text that already fits doesn't rewrap.

Why it helps. Opening the sidebar is a moment when you want to keep your place. A measure narrower than the column absorbs the change. Centering it would move it sideways instead, so it stays left.

Rules
1 Content is loudest · 10 Calm motion
Ships as
--qs-measure as a max-width on block content

At narrow widths both panes rewrap; the cap only helps while the column stays wider than the measure.

06

Overlay, don't insert

Page actions (add tags, configure) float at the right of the title line instead of holding a row of their own. They still fade in on hover exactly as now.

Why it helps. A whispered row still takes a full line, so the page starts lower than it needs to. Giving the space back once, statically, means revealing the actions can never push anything down.

Rules
2 Fade, never remove · 9 Shape over chrome
Ships as
position: absolute on .ls-page-title-actions inside the title wrapper

Both traces are flat. Compare where the first block starts.

07

Tabular figures for numbers

Property values and table cells use equal-width digits, as dates already do. When a value changes from 48 to 11, whatever follows it stays put.

Why it helps. A "1" is narrower than other digits in most interface fonts. In a daily log, every new entry nudges the line sideways a little.

Rules
6 Small but reliable · 10 Calm motion
Ships as
font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums in meta.properties and meta.views

The movement is a few pixels at a time. It's small, but it happens every time a value changes.

How hover reveals

Fewer, bigger targets, so a passing pointer changes the page less often.

08

One hover zone per block

Today the tag row and the properties of a block each reveal on their own hover. Proposed: resting anywhere on a block's own row reveals its metadata together, and children don't count.

Why it helps. Moving the pointer across a block now turns things on and off several times. One zone per block changes volume once on the way in and once on the way out. It pairs with proposal 2's dwell delay.

Rules
